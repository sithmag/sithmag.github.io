<section class="clinical-motivation" id="personal-motivation">
  <h2>Personal Connection to Biomedical Engineering</h2>
    My interest in biomedical engineering was also shaped by my own experience with injury, rehabilitation, and surgical care. After learning I was diagnosed with <strong>hypermobility</strong> and experiencing <strong>multiple knee dislocations</strong> I underwent <strong>MPFL reconstruction</strong>. This made me become much more aware of how medical devices, surgical planning, rehabilitation tools, and assistive technologies can directly influence a person’s mobility, independence, and quality of life.
<br>
<br>
    Seeing the impact of orthopaedic care and rehabilitation from a patient perspective gave me a stronger appreciation for biomedical engineering as a practical field, not just a technical one. It showed me that good health technology needs to be reliable, usable, comfortable, and designed around real people. This experience continues to influence my interest in <strong>assistive technology</strong>, <strong>medical devices</strong>, <strong>rehabilitation engineering</strong>, and hands-on engineering work that improves patient outcomes.
<br>
</section>

<section class="clinical-intro" id="clinical-intro">
  <h2>Clinical Exposure: Sri Lanka & Australia</h2  >
<br>
    A short record of my early clinical exposure in both <strong>Sri Lanka</strong> and <strong>Australia</strong>, and how it shaped my interest in <strong>healthcare engineering</strong>, <strong>patient-centred design</strong>, and the real-world constraints that influence clinical outcomes.
<br>
<br>
    Rather than presenting these experiences as clinical training, I include them as formative context. They helped me understand how hospital systems operate, how resource constraints change decision-making, and why health technology needs to be designed for the environments it will actually be used in.
</section>

<section class="clinical-card" id="karapitiya-oncology">
  <div class="clinical-info">
    <h2>Teaching Hospital Karapitiya — Oncology Unit</h2>
    <p><strong>Karapitiya, Sri Lanka</strong> · <em>Jan 2020</em></p>


<h3>Overview</h3>
<p>
  Completed a supervised secondary student work experience placement within the <strong>Oncology Unit</strong>, gaining early exposure to clinical environments in a high-acuity public hospital setting.
</p>

<h3>What I did / observed</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Completed <strong>38 hours of supervised observation</strong> across ward rounds, clinics, and theatre sessions</li>
  <li>Observed oncological procedures and multidisciplinary cancer care pathways</li>
  <li>Assisted with basic ward administrative tasks and patient-flow support</li>
  <li>Gained exposure to endoscopy and diagnostic workflows</li>
  <li>Built early awareness of ethics, privacy, and patient dignity within healthcare delivery</li>
</ul>

<h3>Engineering relevance</h3>
<p>
  This experience gave me early insight into how clinical workflows, patient dignity, equipment access, and hospital resource constraints influence the way healthcare technologies need to be designed, implemented, and supported.
</p>

<section class="clinical-learnings" id="clinical-learnings">
  <h2>Key Learnings from Clinical Exposure</h2>

  <div class="learning-grid">
    <div class="learning-card">
      <h3>Health technology has a human impact</h3>
      <p>
        My own experience with injury, surgery, and rehabilitation showed me how strongly medical devices, assistive tools, and clinical decision-making can affect mobility, confidence, independence, and quality of life.
      </p>
    </div>


<div class="learning-card">
  <h3>Quality of life care extends beyond clinical decisions</h3>
  <p>
    These experiences highlighted the role of biomedical engineering in designing technologies that prioritise usability, comfort, and patient dignity, not just technical performance.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="learning-card">
  <h3>Resource constraints shape healthcare delivery</h3>
  <p>
    They reinforced the importance of engineering solutions that are cost-effective, robust, and adaptable, particularly in settings where ideal infrastructure or equipment is unavailable.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="learning-card">
  <h3>Strong workflows sustain outcomes under pressure</h3>
  <p>
    They showed me that biomedical engineering solutions need to integrate smoothly into existing clinical workflows, supporting clinicians rather than adding complexity or burden.
  </p>
</div>


  </div>
</section>
